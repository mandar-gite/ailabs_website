## Background

In rural India's vernacular-medium schools, the English teacher often isn't fluent in English. They're most at home in their own language, and they use it to teach English.

## The problem

Teachers needed help while they ran their classes, and the NGO didn't have the staff to give it.

## The client

A national education NGO with a method for teaching English through the language a teacher knows best. It trains teacher-entrepreneurs across rural India to use it.

## The challenge

A teacher is mid-class and gets stuck: a point in the lesson, an admin rule, a child who won't settle. Some of these teachers are trained. Many aren't. Each question has to be understood and answered correctly, and it has to happen while the class is still running.

The usual fix is trained staff in the field or in a call centre. The NGO didn't have enough of those people, and hiring more would have been expensive.

A separate app wasn't the answer either. Teachers might not download it, and if they did, they might not open it.

## The solution

LeapBuddy runs as a web chat today, so there's nothing to install. WhatsApp comes next, because the teachers already use it every day. The NGO runs no extra infrastructure for it.

The main choice was what LeapBuddy is allowed to answer from. It uses only the NGO's own material: the programme FAQ, teaching content, worksheets, training videos and support guides. It never answers from outside sources. That limits what it can say, and it means every answer is one the NGO already approved. When a teacher needs a worksheet or a video, LeapBuddy sends it in the chat.

## The impact

LeapBuddy's pilot is under way, with teachers next. It's built to answer questions on content, admin and class management while the class is running, so the NGO can support more teachers without a bigger field or call-centre team.

Teachers won't need to install anything or learn a new tool. When the NGO's material doesn't cover a question, LeapBuddy says so and doesn't guess.

**Want to know how it's built?** The technical detail is on the [LeapBuddy project page](/projects/leapbuddy/).

## A takeaway for us all

Help works best inside a tool people already open every day. Limit what it can say to material the organisation has already approved, and it becomes easier to trust.

## Here's what you can do

- List the questions your field or support team answers again and again.
- Gather the material those answers already come from: FAQs, guides, worksheets, videos.
- Decide what the assistant must never answer, and what happens when it can't.
- Start with a small group, and measure before you scale.
