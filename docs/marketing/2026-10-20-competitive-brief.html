Competitive brief

72° AI LABS and 8 Indian AI firms

2 seeds you named (Cere Labs, DataChamps), 4 lookalikes and 2 larger benchmarks, compared on positioning, proof, price and content.

Research date: 20 Oct 2026 Sources: company sites, directories, news Status: draft for agency briefing

Executive summary

The field splits in 2. Enterprise players (Exponentia, Talentica, Cogniquest, Cere Labs) carry logos, partner badges and certifications. SME-facing players (DataChamps, Magenta, FutureSmart, Agility Tech) carry thin proof and quiet or mass-produced content.

Nobody in the set says "Tally" or "GST" on their homepage, and nobody publishes a price. 72ai already has both raw ingredients: a Tally project with a measured result and a public engagement model with weeks attached.

Biggest opportunity

Own "custom AI on your Tally books" for mid-sized Indian firms. The space between enterprise doc-AI platforms and dormant SME dashboard tools is empty, and FastAccounting's 95.9% figure is the proof nobody else has.

Biggest threat

Speed claims are getting commoditised. Agility Tech says "Production AI in 3-8 Weeks" and Talentica's DevX AI Pods claim 50% faster delivery. 72ai's 2-3 week PoC stops standing out unless it comes with named, measured results.

Positioning map

Horizontal: who they sell to. Vertical: whether the buyer gets a product or a build. Placement is a judgement from each company's own site, plotted on one scale.

72ai shares its quadrant with FutureSmart (overseas clients, tutorial-led) and Agility Tech (UK and global, very broad offer). Neither anchors to Indian books or Indian buyers.

Competitor profiles

The overlap tag rates how often 72ai would meet each firm in a real deal for its target buyer, a mid-sized Indian B2B firm.

Seeds

Cere Labs

Overlap: medium
Mumbai · founded 2015 · about 50 people · bootstrapped · cerelabs.com
What they do
Enterprise GenAI with Queryfic, a document Q&A product, sold by industry (legal, pharma, financial services).
Proof
Logos read as Standard Chartered, HDFC Bank, Capgemini, Lodha. Forbes India DGEMS 2025 Select 200. No case studies; the numbers on the site are Forrester and BCG stats.
Content
Articles section is empty. The founder writes for KDnuggets and has a book on GPT.
Where they're weak
No published results, no content engine, enterprise-only sales motion.

Read: the closest rival to DocIntel, and also Mumbai-based. 72ai wins where the buyer wants results on its own documents, shown with numbers.

DataChamps

Overlap: low
Nashik · founded 2021 · $500K seed (Windrose, Jun 2024) · datachamps.ai
What they do
No-code analytics platform with industry KPIs, an AI chat layer and BI analyst services. Part of the Ruby Consulting group.
Proof
3 unnamed case studies, testimonials with no company names. Press names Kotak, Nivea, SIDBI as clients (unverified).
Content
6 undated posts, footer reads 2023. Nothing published in the last 3 months.
Where they're weak
Stale site, no Tally mention, no pricing, no named proof.

Read: sells dashboards rather than builds. It overlaps with Report Copilot and only meets 72ai when a buyer is choosing between a tool and a custom build.

Topics 72ai covers that nobody else in the set does: Tally books, GST-adjacent finance classification, handwritten exam sheets, call-audio compliance, and PII-free report generation. Each is a page someone could rank for.

Formats to adopt: numbers-first case studies (Exponentia's cadence), a security one-pager (Cogniquest), and a Clutch profile with 3-5 reviews (Talentica). Skip high-volume news commentary; Agility shows how cheap it looks.

Opportunities

  • The Tally gap. No firm here names Tally or GST. From the SEO review, aiaccountant.com and suvit.io do, but they're products. A custom build on the client's own Tally data is unclaimed.
  • Sub-$25K projects. Talentica's floor is $25,000 and Exponentia sells platform estates. A free Discovery plus a 2-3 week PoC fits budgets they can't serve profitably.
  • Proof is scarce in the SME half. DataChamps, Magenta and Cere Labs show logos with no numbers. 72ai's measured results (95.9% on 5 companies' books) stand out the moment they're on a proper case study page.
  • Ownership. Platforms (Cogniquest, Magenta, DataChamps) rent software. "The client owns what we build" is a clean counter for owners wary of lock-in.
  • The founder is the brand. Cere Labs and FutureSmart sell through their founders. 72ai can too, aimed at Indian CFOs and owners rather than developers.

Threats

  • Speed is now a common claim. "3-8 weeks" (Agility) and "50% faster" (Talentica) sit on the same shelf as 72ai's PoC timeline.
  • Certifications block regulated buyers. Cogniquest's SOC 2 and ISO 27001 badges will win insurance and banking document work before 72ai is shortlisted.
  • Enterprise firms are packaging down. DevX AI Pods and Exponentia's industry agents are productised offers that could reach upper mid-market buyers at fixed prices.
  • Funded SME tools. DataChamps ($500K) and Cogniquest ($1.2M) can outspend a studio on demos and sales, even with weak content today.

Recommended actions

Each one is aimed at the agency target: 8 qualified meetings a month by end of March 2027.

  1. This week: rebuild FastAccounting as a full case study. Put "Tally" in the title, show the 95.9% figure with the before-and-after workflow, and add a clear call to book a Discovery. Today the project pages run 73-213 words.
  2. This week: state a PoC price band on the engagement page. Nobody in this set shows a rupee figure. Use your own number; this brief doesn't propose one.
  3. This month: ship 1 segment landing page, "Custom AI on your Tally data", for mid-sized trading, distribution and CA-led firms. Magenta and DataChamps prove the demand and leave the build option open.
  4. This quarter: publish 1 numbers-first case study a month and open a Clutch profile with 3-5 client reviews. That's the proof layer the agency's outbound will need.
  5. This quarter: write a 1-page data-security note (runs in the client's cloud or on-prem, who sees what, PII handling from Report Copilot). It answers the certification gap without an audit spend yet.

Caveats

  • Future Lab (the third seed) couldn't be identified and isn't in this brief. Cognub was dropped: the registry puts it in Trivandrum, its site didn't load, and its claims had a single source.
  • Headcounts, funding and client names marked as directory data aren't verified. Client names read from logos may be wrong.
  • Agility Tech's Mumbai and BFSI framing came from directories and didn't match its own site.
  • Review sites (G2, GoodFirms, Tracxn) and LinkedIn were barely checked. The overlap tags are judgement calls.
